## Verdict (quotable)
"Twenty-five years of building in Hartford County and I've never once hit the bottom of a homeowner's 'range' on a 1965 house — and this draft's bottoms are fiction. The Phase 2 headline of $75–160K contradicts the draft's own line-item budget, which sums to $87–151K in hard costs before the 15–25% soft costs the same page tells you to add. ## Biggest catch
Phase 2 refutes its own number on the same screen: 12 line items sum to $87K/$151K hard costs; +15–25% soft costs → $100K–$189K all-in; yet the headline says $75–160K. $75K is 14% below the draft's own hard-cost floor. Honest range for an 18×18 elevated-foundation room with engineered rear-wall opening + deck rebuild: **$110–185K all-in** (~$340–570/sf on 324 sf). The LVL/steel header + posts + shoring ($6–12K) appears in NO line item.

## Corrections demanded
1. Phase 2: $75–160K → **$110–185K all-in**; add 13th line item "engineered header + posts + temporary shoring, $6–12K"; elevated foundation $15–25K is thin → **$22–35K** (7.5–8-ft walls on ~54 lf ≈ full foundation).
2. Phase 1: $25–50K → **$32–60K**, and GATE the AC price on a duct inspection — "existing forced-air ducts" is an INFERENCE from the assessor/MLS heat discrepancy, stated as fact (violates the data-integrity rule; load-bearing for the whole Phase-1 price). If ducts absent/heat-only-sized: ducted CAC from scratch or whole-house mini-splits **$15–30K**.
3. **$8–15K "1965 allowance"** missing: likely 100-amp service → 200-amp upgrade ($2.5–5K) triggered by a 3-ton condenser in Phase 1; aluminum branch-wiring era starts ~1965 ($1.5–8K if present + insurance issue); probable 9×9 vinyl-asbestos tile in the workshop corner ($1.5–5K); pre-1978 lead-safe RRP (+5–10% demo labor).

Roof tie-in as drawn doesn't work at $8–14K: main floor ~4 ft above grade + 8-ft ceiling puts addition structure above the existing ~12–12.5-ft eave — either ceiling drops toward IRC R305 7-ft minimum or tie-in moves above the eave (strip shingles, sister rafters, re-flash). Carry **$12–20K** or state the ceiling compromise.
